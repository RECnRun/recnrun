# E4: Stop and restart control

Task P1.3 of [PLAN.md](../PLAN.md#p1-minimal-service-worker-proof-source-5-14-deliverable-2). Spec: `tests/experiments/E4-stop-restart.spec.ts`, run by `vp run test:experiments`. Result: Google Chrome 156.0.8078.79 on Linux in Docker, load method `cdp-load-unpacked`, 2026-11-03, run A `2026-11-03T08-08-23.751Z-b1dcc335` (E4 alone, 62 samples in 16.1 min) and run B `2026-11-03T08-26-58.988Z-ab797a85` (the whole layer, 8 passed in 17.1 min; E4 16.2 min). This report is written by hand from the runs' `verdicts.json` and `samples.json`; where the runs differ, both values are given as A / B. Nothing here is validated on macOS or Windows yet.

## Hypotheses and verdicts

Each sample registers `sw-main.js` under a tab Route A armed, applies one mechanism, waits 3 s with nothing that could start the worker, then fires one trigger with a restart marker armed. It passes only when the worker stopped (Chrome's `Target.getTargets` stops listing its target within the bound; for `debugger-pause`, `Debugger.paused` arrived and the worker made no request until released), nothing started it again before the trigger, and any restart after the trigger was seen before its first statement: the first `/me?from=<script>` request carries `__rec=<restart marker>`, and Route A's auto-attach or the isolator's restart watch bootstrapped and released the worker. The worker of every sample made no request between the stop and the trigger, so the ledger alone cannot tell a stopped worker from an idle one; the target list can.

## How it runs

The isolator owns every `chrome.*` call. `apps/isolator/src/background/stop/` exposes `recnrunStop` on its service worker; each method checks its request with a `valibot` schema, refuses a target, tab, or origin outside the loopback before any command, and returns a journal of every `chrome.debugger`, `chrome.declarativeNetRequest`, and `chrome.tabs` call with its result or Chrome's exact error and `performance.now()` timestamps:

- `closeTarget({ targetId, via })` sends `Target.closeTarget` on a session of the worker's own, or on the tab session (`via: "tab"`).
- `terminateExecution({ targetId })` sends `Runtime.terminateExecution` on the worker's session.
- `pauseHold({ targetId })` attaches, sends `Debugger.enable` and `Debugger.pause`, and forces `Runtime.evaluate("1")` so an idle worker has a statement to pause on; it waits up to 5 s for `Debugger.paused`. `release` sends `Debugger.resume`, `Debugger.disable`, and detaches.
- `blockScope({ origin })` adds a session rule that blocks `^<origin>/sw-[^/?#]*\.js`, then evicts every window client: `client.navigate("about:blank")` evaluated in the worker, then `chrome.tabs.remove` for the tabs left. `unblock()` removes the rules.
- `unregister({ tabId })` evaluates `registration.unregister()` in the tab; `client({ tabId, action })` evaluates a fixed expression there: the registration and its sync tags, a `postMessage` to the controller with a 5 s reply bound, `registration.update()`, or `registration.sync.register(tag)`.
- `watch({ marker })` records `Inspector.targetCrashed`, `Inspector.targetReloadedAfterCrash`, and attach and detach events on every session the isolator holds and, on `targetReloadedAfterCrash`, sends `Runtime.evaluate` of `withMarker(marker)` and `Runtime.runIfWaitingForDebugger` on that session, as Route A does on attach; `unwatch()` returns the events.

The spec only drives pages and reads results. Each sample opens a new tab, arms Route A on it with a marker unique to the sample, opens `sw.html?sw=<classic|module>`, waits for the page's report, its `sw:first-paint` entry, and the worker's `sw:sync:fixture-sync` entry, and takes the worker's target id from `Target.getTargets`. It applies the mechanism, disarms the tab (except `held`), polls `Target.getTargets` until the target is gone (bound 10 s; 3 s for `terminate-execution`; 40 s for `dnr-block-evict`, past Chrome's 30 s idle timeout), and waits 3 s. The trigger then rotates through:

- navigation: a new tab, armed with the restart marker, opens `/offline.html` under the scope with an 8 s bound;
- `postMessage`: the first tab, armed with the restart marker, posts to its controller through `client`;
- Background Sync: the fixture's network cut, a sync tag registered through `client`, a 5 s wait for its `sw:sync:<tag>` entry, and the network restored. The cut drops requests at the fixture only and leaves Chrome online, so the event fired at once in every sample whose worker was not paused.

`dnr-block-evict` and `unregister` close every client, which leaves no page to post a message or register a sync from, so they use only the navigation trigger. `terminate-execution`, which Chrome refused, has no trigger. `debugger-pause` fires the trigger while the worker is held, then releases it and waits up to 5 s for a held navigation to finish. After the trigger the spec waits up to 8 s for a fresh `/me?from=<script>` request, disarms the armed tab (Route A's journal shows what its auto-attach saw), reads the registration status and Cache Storage through a tab still on the origin (`takeSnapshot`), and, for `dnr-block-evict`, calls `registration.update()`. It then clears the origin (`Storage.clearDataForOrigin` through Route A), closes its tabs, closes every remaining worker target, and waits until `Target.getTargets` lists none. The pure evaluation lives in `tests/experiments/e4-samples.ts`, tested by `tests/e4-samples.test.ts`; the helpers E2 and E4 share are in `tests/experiments/fixture-pages.ts`. `samples.json` keeps every sample with the isolator's journal, the target id, the trigger's result, the first restart request, Route A's entries, the watch's events, and the side effects.

## Observations

- **`Target.closeTarget` stops the worker at once, and every trigger starts it again.** In 29 of 29 samples of each run, through the worker's session or the tab's, Chrome answered `success: true`; the isolator's `Target.getTargets` right after the reply still listed the target, and the spec's poll stopped seeing it within 25 ms (p50 7). Nothing started the worker during the 3 s that followed. Each of the three triggers started it again, within 18 ms in run A and 145 ms in run B (p50 10 and 13). The registration, its Background Sync support (the sync tags were empty again once the event fired), and the `fixture-shell-v1` precache survived in all 58 samples; no worker script was fetched.
- **A restart keeps the old target id, and Route A's auto-attach does not see it start.** After a navigation (6 samples of `classic`, `module`, and `tab-session` per run), Route A's auto-attach on the newly armed tab attached the restarted worker under the target id it had before the stop, with `waitingForDebugger: false`, after the worker's first request had gone out without the marker; the bootstrap still answered with the restart marker. After a `postMessage` or a sync tag, with the first tab armed again, Route A attached nothing (10 of 10 per run).
- **Keeping the session across the stop sees every restart, but a classic script usually runs first.** With Route A's tab session kept (`held`, `held-module`), the isolator's session on the worker received `Inspector.targetReloadedAfterCrash` in 26 of 26 restarts, unasked and without `Inspector.enable`; the watch's bootstrap answered with the restart marker and the release succeeded every time. The first request carried the marker in 6 of 6 module workers but in only 2 of 20 classic ones (run A, both after a navigation; none in run B). In the 18 others the first request reached the ledger 1.1 to 7.5 ms after the reload event and 0.5 to 2.7 ms before the bootstrap's `Runtime.evaluate` returned (the fixture's clock and the isolator's are compared through `performance.timeOrigin`, so a millisecond or two is within error), and the later Background Sync entry carried the restart marker: the bootstrap ran, after the first statement.
- **`Runtime.terminateExecution` is refused** to the extension: `{"code":-32000,"message":"Runtime.terminateExecution is not allowed for untrusted clients"}` in 3 of 3 per run, and the target stayed listed.
- **`Debugger.pause` holds the worker, and its clients wait.** `Debugger.paused` arrived in 10 of 10 per run (the command took p50 3 and 5 ms), and the worker made no request while held. A navigation under the scope did not finish within 8 s in 4 of 4 and finished after the release. While the worker was held, the navigation's update check still fetched `sw-main.js?variant=classic` (4 of 4). A `postMessage` got no reply within the page's 5 s bound (3 of 3). A sync tag registered while held fired only after the release (3 of 3), its entry carrying the sample's original marker: the same worker went on, it did not restart. The target stayed listed and attached throughout; a pause holds the worker, it does not stop it.
- **The DNR block stops updates, not the worker.** In 10 of 10 per run the rule was added, `client.navigate("about:blank")` threw `TypeError: 'about:blank' is not a valid URL.`, so the isolator closed the tab with `chrome.tabs.remove`, and the target was still listed and attached 40 s later. `registration.update()` then failed with `TypeError: Failed to update a ServiceWorker for scope ('http://app.localhost:4600/') with script ('http://app.localhost:4600/sw-main.js?variant=classic'): An unknown error occurred when fetching the script.`, and no script fetch reached the fixture. A navigation from a new armed tab reached the same, still running worker: Route A attached it with `waitingForDebugger: false`, the bootstrap answered with the sample's original marker, and no fresh start was logged.
- **`unregister()` removes the registration, not the running worker.** In 10 of 10 per run the tab had no registration or controller afterwards, while the worker's target was still listed and attached 10 s after its only client closed; a navigation from a new tab, which no worker controlled, still auto-attached it running. The precache survived in 10 of 10. The isolator's own attach to the tab failed with `Another debugger is already attached to the tab with id: <tabId>.` because Route A held it, and the evaluation ran on Route A's session instead.
- **Inferred: the harness keeps a second DevTools client on every service worker.** playwright-core 1.64.1 (`lib/coreBundle.js`), which `connectOverCDP` runs, sets `Target.setAutoAttach({ autoAttach: true, waitForDebuggerOnStart: true, flatten: true })` on the browser session, wraps each `service_worker` target in a `CRServiceWorker`, and sends `Runtime.runIfWaitingForDebugger` on attach and again on every `Inspector.targetReloadedAfterCrash`; `Target.getTargets` reported `attached: true` for every listed worker in these runs. Inferred from Chromium's source and not verified here: a service worker version with DevTools attached is exempt from the idle timeout (`ServiceWorkerVersion::SetDevToolsAttached`), a doomed version waits for DevTools to detach before it stops (`stop_when_devtools_detached_`), and `ServiceWorkerDevToolsManager` keeps the agent host of a stopped worker while a session is attached and reuses it when the worker starts again (`WorkerStopped`, `WorkerStarting`, `WorkerRestarted`), which sends `Inspector.targetReloadedAfterCrash` to the existing sessions instead of a new `Target.attachedToTarget`. Together they would explain why the DNR block and `unregister()` never stopped a worker, why a restart kept its target id and was invisible to auto-attach, and why most classic restarts ran before the isolator's bootstrap: Playwright releases the worker as soon as its own event arrives, and the isolator's event and commands go through the extension first. E4 therefore measures these mechanisms in a browser with a second debugger on every worker, which users' browsers do not have.
- **E2's reading of listed targets needs a correction.** [E2](E2.md#observations) concluded that a listed service-worker target is not evidence of a running worker. Here the listed workers that were probed were running: in the DNR samples the worker answered a later bootstrap with the marker it got at registration, 40 s after its only client closed, and in the `unregister` samples the doomed worker auto-attached with `waitingForDebugger: false`. Inferred: the stale targets E2 counted were kept running by the same second client, not merely kept in the list.

## Not covered

Push as a trigger (the spec denies notifications, so no subscription exists; P1.2 covers waking by push), the effect on push subscriptions, a browser without the harness's own DevTools session (Chrome's idle stop, a doomed worker's stop, and a restart that auto-attach sees as new are untested), a `Debugger.pause` held long enough for Chrome's request timeouts, `debugger-pause` on a module worker, the isolator's own service worker stopping while it holds a pause or a watch, more samples of `held-module` (3 per run), and Chrome on a desktop or outside Linux.

## Reproducing

`vp run test:experiments -- tests/experiments/E4-stop-restart.spec.ts` (about 16 minutes) appends the five verdicts to `.artifacts/evidence/<run>/verdicts.json`, merges `stopControl` into `.artifacts/evidence/<run>/capabilities.json`, and writes `samples.json` under `.artifacts/evidence/<run>/E4-stop-restart/` (inside `.artifacts/docker/<run>/` for a Docker run).
