## Question

§1.1 and P3.5: while the Default, bound as master, is closed, do its workers keep running and keep their binding, and refuse anyone who takes the master's place, whether a same-user forger under the Default's id or a third profile that records itself as master? When the same Default comes back on its root, does it get its binding, the workers' status, and the work that ran on back, with no second execution and no new pairing? And can a reader of the verdicts tell that case from a worker restart?

## Method

E6 changes no RECnRun code. The harness (`tests/browser/fixtures/master-restart.ts`, shared with `tests/browser/master-restart.spec.ts`) starts the Default, Worker 1, and Worker 2 on throwaway roots of their own that share one Downloads folder, with the stand-in launcher of E5 rerunning `catalog.sh` every 2 s; once the three are linked over Downloads, `bindHierarchy` (the e2e fixture of P3.4, standing in for the launcher record and the P5.3 bootstrap) records the Default as master of both and each worker's binding to the Default's identity key. Quits are SIGTERM and relaunches start Chrome on the same root and install again (group 9), as in E5. "Detached" is E5's: the extension's pages and Playwright's connection closed. Every sample starts fresh roots and a fresh Downloads folder and removes them afterwards.

One round trip feeds four cells, each judged on its own phase: the Default's dashboard starts a session in Worker 1 (`session-start`, the request its **Record** button sends), the Default quits, both workers run detached for 75 s (one presence lifetime and 15 s), the harness attaches again, and the same Default comes back on its root. E6.a.forgedMaster plays a virtual peer of E5 (`tests/experiments/fixtures/e5-virtual.ts`) under the Default's profile id, with a certificate and identity key of its own. E6.a.thirdMaster launches a real third profile (`Intruder`) that links with both workers, then writes its own `local:role` as master with both workers in `local:boundWorkers`, and `role: "master"` into its presence, as a same-user program could; then it sends, over its verified links, a `SESSION_START` with a `role` field, an `ACTIVATE_TAB`, and a `SESSION_START`. E6.c.workerRestart quits and relaunches Worker 2 instead, with the master left running.

E6 reads each profile's `local:role`, `local:boundWorkers`, and `local:peerPins` (the recorded hierarchy); Worker 1's `session:recording` and `session:remoteController` (the work and who started it); `session:peerLinks`; the `status` the master's background answers its dashboard; each profile's background log; the files on disk and their mtime; each profile's download history, to count signal writes; and `Target.getTargets` over a CDP client that attaches to nothing, to tell whether Chrome lists the worker. Worker-to-worker control is not repeated here: `tests/browser/hierarchy.spec.ts` (P3.4) refuses it over a valid link.

## Work in progress: the policy the code supports

The simplest policy the current code supports, with no queue: **the executor keeps the work it was given, and its state waits in the executor.** A session the master started in a worker keeps recording while the master is closed; its state lives in that worker's `session:recording`, and the master that started it in `session:remoteController`. Nothing is queued anywhere: every command is a request over a live link and is answered over it, so no command and no result is in flight once the link is down. While the worker holds no verified link to the profile in `session:remoteController`, its `status` reports `controlledBy: null` (`background/peer-requests.ts`), so the worker's own dashboard shows the session's controls and the person at that profile can stop it. The controller record stays: when the same Default links again, the worker reports `SESSION_STATUS`, the Default shows the session as its own, and its next command (`session-stop`) is accepted as the controller's (`background/executor.ts`), with no second `SESSION_START`. By code reading, not exercised: a worker restart ends its session, since session storage ends with the browser; a session the worker's own person stops while the master is away is gone when the master returns.

## Observation

Lab figures are the full run's three samples; the smoke run gave the same verdicts with figures of the same order (relink 1287 ms after the relaunch, the forger refused after 721 and 983 ms, the intruder linked after 3720 ms, Worker 2 back after 2391 ms).

- E6.a.absent: both workers showed the Default gone 2590 to 3363 ms after the quit; `catalog.sh` dropped its presence 57.6 to 59.9 s after the quit, one TTL after its last write. Detached for 75 s, each worker rewrote its presence 3 times, longest gaps 30002 to 30005 ms, and Chrome listed both workers throughout. Each kept `role: worker`, its binding, and its pins; the two kept their link; Worker 1's `session:recording` and `session:remoteController` were unchanged, and its `status` reported `controlledBy: null`.
- E6.a.forgedMaster: each worker logged the refusal 420 to 1367 ms after the forged files landed (the offerer `no offer to <id>: its certificate is signed by another identity than the one pinned`, the answerer `Refused a code from <id>: its pinned identity did not sign its certificate.`). No answer to the forged offers and no offer to the forger was written, no link came up, and no record changed. The real Default, relaunched, linked with both again in 1.1 to 2.2 s.
- E6.a.thirdMaster: the intruder linked with both workers 1.1 to 2.6 s after its first presence. Each worker dropped its frame with a `role` field (`dropped an invalid frame`), logged `REFUSED ACTIVATE_TAB from <id>: not the bound master` and the same for `SESSION_START`, switched no tab, and started no session. Each worker pinned the intruder at their first meeting, as any profile of the same user is pinned; its `role`, `boundWorkers`, and pin of the Default did not change.
- E6.b.relink: the relaunched Default wrote a presence with a new boot and the same profile id, and linked with both workers 1499 to 3518 ms after the relaunch (1279 to 3148 ms after that presence). No profile's role, binding, or pins changed: the pins of P3.3 took the Default back with no pairing code and no gesture.
- E6.b.status: the Default's first `status` after the link answered Worker 1's session as `{ status: "recording", mine: true, live: true }` (11 to 17 ms after its dashboard asked), no session in Worker 2, both links verified, and Worker 1's tabs listed the session's tab.
- E6.b.results: Worker 1's log had no `received SESSION_START` after the relaunch, its session was the one the Default started, and the Default's `session-stop` was accepted and the session gone 2 to 7 ms later.
- E6.c.workerRestart: the Default showed Worker 2 gone 2809 to 3613 ms after its quit and linked with its new boot 2312 to 2341 ms after the relaunch, while its own boot stayed and its link with Worker 1 held in every one of 21 to 24 polls. Neither record changed, and the Default's `activate` switched the tab in the restarted worker. The verdicts tell the cases apart: here `masterBootKept` and `steadyLinkDrops: 0`; in E6.b.relink the Default's `newBoot`.

## Answer to the P3.5 question

> Yes, on Google Chrome 157 in the Linux lab and 156 on the macOS host, for unpacked installs over one Downloads root: while the Default, bound as master, is closed, its workers keep running past its presence's lifetime, keep their role, binding, and pins, keep the link between them and the session it started, and give that session's controls to their own person. Neither a same-user forger under the Default's id with another key nor a third profile that records itself as master gets a link to the Default's place or any command run. The same Default, relaunched on its root, links with both again within 3.9 s under the pins it had, with no code and no gesture, shows the session as its own and live at once, and stops it with one command; nothing ran twice. A worker restart is a different trace: the master keeps its boot and its other link, and the worker comes back bound within 2.7 s. Long absences, a master crash, and a command in flight when the master quits were not measured.

## Limits of this measurement

- The binding is written by `bindHierarchy`, the stand-in for the launcher record and the P5.3 bootstrap; E6 measures what the profiles do with it, not how it is made.
- The work is a recording session with no steps: nothing is typed into its tab while the master is away. No command was in flight when the master quit; every command is answered over the live link, and a quit cannot be timed between a command's arrival and its answer from the test.
- The master quits with SIGTERM and is away 75 s, one presence lifetime and 15 s. Longer absences rest on the presence alarm that E5.a.closePages measured for 125 s; a master crash (SIGKILL) was not produced.
- The intruder writes its own role into its storage over CDP, as a same-user program could; a clone that carries the template's identity key is P5.3's case (see the P3.3 note on `discardIdentityKey()`).
- Installs are diagnostic (`Extensions.loadUnpacked` over CDP); packed installs and Windows were not executed. The host's Default is the Default of a throwaway root, never the personal profile.
- The harness gives Chrome its own download behaviour back after every `attach` as at launch, since Playwright's `connectOverCDP` routes every download to its own folder again. Before that fix a relaunched Default could not read Worker 1's answer, which had gone to Playwright's folder; E5's cells that attach only checked links already up, and pass with the fix (`2026-11-04T11-52-43.831Z-ff7ef4c9-b081-4d72-9ec1-e8c6e7f6a4e3`).

## What it means for the plan

- P3.5's work-in-progress policy is the one above: the executor keeps its work and its state, nothing is queued, and the worker's own person gets the controls while the master is away. A different policy (pause the session, or keep the controls with the master) would be an owner decision the plan does not make yet.
- A worker restart ends the session it ran, by code reading: if work must survive an executor's restart, the session has to move to persistent storage; the plan does not settle whether it must.
- No change to PLAN.md, CHROMIUM.md, or ARCHITECTURE.md: E6 added no folder or boundary, and the Chromium groups it relied on behaved as recorded.
