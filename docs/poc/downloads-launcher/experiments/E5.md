## Question

§14 question 1, for the handshake and the mesh: "Downloads + `file://` substituem o FSA sem seletor/grant de escrita?" Once each profile writes its presence through `chrome.downloads` and the launcher catalogs it (P2), do the profiles of one OS user pair and keep the mesh of PR #19 by themselves, with no folder picker, no write grant, no pairing code, no page open, and no click, and do the files they exchange hold up against stale, repeated, damaged, and badly stamped copies and against profiles that quit or crash?

## Method

The extension (`poc-d0`) gains one trigger (`background/downloads-pairing.ts`): every presence a profile writes once Chrome told it its Downloads root starts the folder flow of PR #19, or starts it again, with no page open. The offscreen document then reads `registry.json` and each listed peer's presence and signal files through `file://` with `cache: "no-store"` every second (`POLL_MS`), and writes signals through the background's relay, in the layout P3.1 fixed (decision 11). Of each pair the lower `profileId` offers, with ICE gathered completely before it writes (`gatherTimeoutMs` 10 s); the other answers each offer whose `pairingId` it has not answered yet; the offerer offers whenever it holds no unexpired offer for that peer (`PAIRING_TTL_MS`, 5 min), connects with the answer to the one it holds, and waits `RETRY_MS` (30 s) after a connection whose link did not come up before it offers again. Nothing else changed in the pairing, the mesh, or the trust model.

The harness (`tests/browser/fixtures/downloads-profiles.ts`) starts every profile on a throwaway root of its own, gives Chrome its own download behaviour back (`Browser.setDownloadBehavior { behavior: "default" }`), and installs `poc-d0` with `Extensions.loadUnpacked`. All the profiles of a sample share one Downloads folder: in the lab Chrome's own default, the container user's `$HOME/Downloads` (nothing written in `Preferences`); on the host a stand-in folder written into each root's `Preferences` before its first launch. A stand-in launcher record names that one root, and the harness runs `catalog.sh` once after every profile's first presence is on disk, then every 2 s; E5 opens no bootstrap page, since the record already names the root (E4 measured discovery). The profiles are named `Default`, then `Worker 1` to `Worker 4` in launch order; E5 gives them no role (P3.4). A quit is SIGTERM and a crash SIGKILL, both to the PID the sample spawned; a relaunch starts Chrome on the same root and installs again, since a CDP install does not survive a relaunch (group 9). "Detached" means the extension's pages closed and Playwright's connection, which attaches to every service worker, closed (group 12); Chrome keeps its other tabs.

E5 reads files on disk and their mtime; each profile's storage through its own worker (`local:authorizedPeers`, `local:profileIdentity`, `local:peerPins`, `session:pendingOffers`, `session:folderActed`); each profile's own download history, where each write is one item whose `data:` URL holds the bytes, to count writes and to date each answer; and `Target.getTargets` over a CDP client that attaches to nothing, to tell whether Chrome lists the worker. No observation carries a path.

Six cells need files no real profile writes. There the test plays a virtual peer (`tests/experiments/fixtures/e5-virtual.ts`): it writes a presence and signals in the extension's exact bytes, each replaced by a rename, with a complete SDP and the certificate fingerprint of a real `RTCPeerConnection` in a blank tab of the Default, so the Default judges them by its own rules and answers with a real answer. Its id is the lowest v4 id, so the Default answers it; in E5.a.duplicate a second one with the highest id receives the Default's offer; a third (`00000000-0000-4000-8000-000000000001`) carries the presences `catalog.sh` must refuse. Each mutation is one write into the throwaway folder, never a change to RECnRun. Every mutation sits between controls, fresh offers the Default must answer within 15 s; a mutation holds when no answer to it came within 6 s, and the sample also fails if the Default's history shows an answer to it later. Worker 1, beside them, must link with the Default.

Samples: 3 full and 1 smoke (lab), 3 (host); E5.a.closePages takes 2, since each of its samples waits two presence lifetimes. Gestures: 0 everywhere. Every sample starts fresh roots and a fresh Downloads folder and removes them afterwards.

## Observation

Lab figures are the full run's three samples (two for E5.a.closePages). The smoke run gave the same verdicts, with figures of the same order (in E5.a.arrival, offer 354 ms and answer 552 ms; at most 902 ms in E5.a.readCache; 3368 ms in E5.b.killExecutor). Host figures, three samples (two for E5.a.closePages), all on Google Chrome 156.0.8078.80 on macOS 27.0.1: offer 80 to 806 ms and answer 105 to 836 ms (E5.a.arrival, link 2 ms later); 2 and 1 candidates, one write each (E5.a.iceComplete); answers 497 to 986 ms (E5.a.previousRun, E5.a.duplicate, E5.a.readCache); the pair linked 1174 to 1288 ms after the first catalog (E5.a.simultaneous); the presence 3 s ahead answered after 3252 to 3396 ms, the other offers after 358 to 1001 ms, with the same refusals and logs as the lab (E5.a.clockChange); relinked 1666 to 2189 ms after both quit (E5.a.bothRestart); exchanged 11.7 s after the kill, again with `earlierOfferAnswers: 2` (E5.a.noPage); 5 rewrites per presence, gaps 30001 to 30003 ms (E5.a.closePages); ten links 2245 to 3004 ms after the first catalog, all through files (E5.b.mesh); the fifth joined in 3062 to 3578 ms (E5.b.fifth); Worker 2 shown gone 2854 to 3049 ms after the kill (E5.b.killExecutor). E5.a.quitDuringIce differs below.

- E5.a.arrival: the answerer trusted the offerer 115 to 139 ms after the offer's mtime, and the offerer took the answer 740 to 786 ms after its mtime; the link was up 5 to 7 ms later. Both delays are a poll phase plus a read, under the 1.5 s bound.
- E5.a.iceComplete: every offer carried 2 candidates and every answer 1; one write each; linked in 5 to 10 ms.
- E5.a.previousRun: all seven checks held in every sample; the answered offers took 478 to 688 ms. A presence of the earlier boot written again after the new one did not bring the earlier boot back.
- E5.a.simultaneous: the first catalog listed both; the lower id wrote one offer, the higher id one answer and no offer; the pair linked 1.9 to 2.1 s after the first catalog.
- E5.a.duplicate: no second answer to the repeated offer (`repeatedAnswers` 0), and the repeated answers left the Default's `session:folderActed` record and its single offer to the high virtual peer unchanged.
- E5.a.expired: the expired offer got no answer; `catalog.sh` logged `catalog.sh: skipped r1/00000000-0000-4000-8000-000000000001: expired` and left the peer out, and its offer got no answer; once it announced again it was listed and answered.
- E5.a.corrupt: the three damaged offers got no answer. A presence with its keys reversed was refused with `expected {"profileId":"`, and its peer's offer got no answer. An offer with its keys reversed was answered (`reorderedAnswered: true`, not a check): the extension parses signals as JSON, so key order is part of the presence contract (decision 11) and not of the signal's.
- E5.a.clockChange: `catalog.sh` refused the presence an hour ahead with `createdAt is ahead of this clock` and the one an hour behind with `expired`; the offer of the one ahead got no answer. The presence 3 s ahead was listed at once, but its offer was answered only after 3481 to 3604 ms, against 553 to 885 ms for the others: the extension refuses a presence whose `expiresAt` is more than `PRESENCE_TTL_MS` from its own clock (`stands` in `p2p/downloads-rendezvous.ts`), with no `CLOCK_SKEW_MS` allowance, so a presence stamped d ahead counts only d later. Two more observations, not checks: an offer whose `expiresAt` is an hour ahead was answered (neither the signal nor the code it carries has an upper bound on its lifetime), and so was one whose `sequence` is an hour ahead; after it, an offer of the same boot whose sequence came back to the clock, as a writer would stamp it after its clock stepped back, got no answer (`heardAfterStampReset: false`) until the peer's next boot.
- E5.a.quitDuringIce: in the lab the kill always came after the answerer had trusted the offerer and before its answer was on disk (`answerOutBeforeKill: false`), and the pair linked 734 to 1230 ms after the kill, with the same profile id. On the host two samples did the same (984 and 1220 ms); in the other the answer was already on disk when the kill landed. The offerer connected with the answer of a dead profile, its link never came up, and it waited `RETRY_MS` before it offered again: the pair linked 32899 ms after the kill, inside the 60 s bound.
- E5.a.bothRestart: new boots on both, the same ids and pins, linked 1250 to 1476 ms after the quit.
- E5.a.readCache: the four versions were answered after 467 to 883 ms.
- E5.a.noPage: after 35 s detached, Chrome still listed both workers. After the kill and an unattended relaunch, the new offer and its answer were on disk 11.4 to 12.4 s after the kill, with Chrome listing both workers; the pair was linked once attached. The relaunched answerer also answered the offer of the earlier link again (`earlierOfferAnswers: 2`), since the record of the offers it answered (`session:folderActed`) ended with its browser and that offer was still valid; the extra answer changed nothing observed.
- E5.a.closePages: each presence was rewritten 5 times in 125 s, longest gaps 30002 to 30004 ms; both workers listed throughout; linked once attached, with no new offer.
- E5.b.mesh: the first catalog listed all five; the ten links formed 1959 to 2283 ms after it, every pair from exactly one offer written by its lower id (`pairsThroughFiles: 10`).
- E5.b.fifth: the four workers were still listed after 35 s detached; the fifth linked with all four 2314 to 4159 ms after its first presence, and the five were linked once attached.
- E5.b.killExecutor: the four others showed Worker 2 gone 3168 to 3297 ms after the kill and kept their six links; it came back with the same id and pins and linked with all four.
- E5.b.disconnectForget: Disconnect showed `Disconnected — <name>` and held 35 s while the others kept their links; Reconnect linked again; Forget removed the line and the trust and held 35 s. The dashboard then had no **Pair with other profiles** button in any sample: `FolderPairing` offers it only while the File System Access folder is granted, which a Downloads build never reports. The request the button sends (`pair-again`) linked the two again (`pairedAgain: true`). Verdict `fail`, a defect of the Downloads builds' dashboard, not of Chrome.

## Prediction check

1. Group 4: every signal Chrome wrote replaced its own path. The Default's answers to the virtual peer, three to five per sample at one path, each carry that path's own name in Chrome's history; a uniquified name would have failed the control, and none did. `saveAs: false` raised no prompt (0 gestures). Holds in the lab and on the host.
2. Group 9: each relaunch installed the build again, as the group predicts; E5 did not check that the previous install was gone first. The extension's storage came back with it: the same `profileId` and pins after every relaunch (E5.a.quitDuringIce, E5.a.bothRestart, E5.b.killExecutor). This agrees with the group's source: `CleanUpCdpInstalledExtensions` calls `DeleteExtensionPrefs`, which removes the extension's entry from the `extensions` preferences and notifies observers. Where Chrome deletes an extension's stored data was not read, so the survival is recorded here as an observation, not in CHROMIUM.md.
3. Group 10 (1): a rewrite is read afresh. Each of four rewrites of one offer path was answered within 467 to 883 ms in the lab and 765 to 985 ms on the host (E5.a.readCache), and no profile ever answered a version it had already answered.
4. Group 12: with no client and no page for 35 s, Chrome still listed every worker (E5.a.noPage, E5.b.fifth). For 125 s the alarm rewrote each presence every 30 s (gaps 30002 to 30004 ms in the lab, 30001 to 30003 ms on the host), as P2.2 measured. ## Answer to §14 question 1, for the handshake and the mesh

> Yes, on Google Chrome 157 in the Linux lab and 156 on the macOS host, for unpacked installs: profiles of one OS user that write through `chrome.downloads` into one Downloads root, and that a launcher catalogs, pair and form the mesh of PR #19 with no folder picker, no write grant, no pairing code, no page open, and no click (0 gestures). Each file is acted on within one 1 s poll; the Default plus one worker link within 2.1 s of the first catalog, and the Default plus four within 2.3 s in the lab and 3 s on the host, each pair from one offer. The layout of P3.1 refuses an earlier boot's signals, lower sequences, repeated offers and answers, expired, truncated, malformed, and self-contradicting files, and presences stamped an hour off. A restarted profile links again within 2.2 s of the quit, and a crashed one within 1.3 s of the kill, or after one 30 s retry period when it crashed after its answer was on disk, keeping its id and pins. A pair whose pages are closed and whose clients are gone keeps its presences fresh and its link up, and links again after a crash with nothing attached. A fifth profile joins four such profiles in under 4.2 s. Disconnect, Reconnect, and Forget hold over Downloads, but after a Forget the Downloads builds' dashboard does not offer to pair again (E5.b.disconnectForget fails). Two stamping limits hold: within one boot, a signal stamped far ahead in sequence silences that writer's later signals until its next boot; and a signal's `expiresAt` has no upper bound. Different roots per profile were not measured; packed installs and Windows were not executed.

## Limits of this measurement

- Clock: neither environment can change its OS clock (the container shares the Docker machine's clock, and the host's is the owner's), so E5.a.clockChange moves the virtual peer's stamps instead. It shows what a profile does with files a clock an hour off would write, not what a profile does when its own clock steps.
- E5.a.quitDuringIce does not choose where in the exchange its kill lands: eight of nine samples killed the answerer before its answer was on disk, and one (host) after. A kill after the offerer connected and before the link came up was not produced.
- One shared Downloads root per sample; pairing across two roots (E4.b.differentRoots) was not measured.
- E5 never opens the bootstrap page. A profile adopts the launcher's token as its boot (P2.1), and the boot is asked on each write (`p2p/downloads-transport.ts`), so an offer written before the adoption carries a boot readers then drop. The offerer keeps that pending offer until it expires (5 min) before it offers again. Not measured.
- The dashboard of the Downloads builds was observed only in E5.b.disconnectForget. By code reading, not exercised, it also shows the File System Access banner (**Choose the RECnRun folder**) and **Let another profile join**, which have no use there.
- The launcher is a Node stand-in that reruns `catalog.sh` every 2 s; P4 sets the real cadence.
- Installs are diagnostic (`Extensions.loadUnpacked` over CDP); the owner's `Load unpacked` gesture is M-P1.a. Packed installs and Windows: not executed.
- The unattended relaunch of E5.a.noPage installs over a raw CDP client opened after Playwright's connection closed. A worker that started while Playwright's connection was closing stalled for about 60 s, with no presence and no offscreen document, until a client attached and detached. This is a harness effect, not explained by the sources read, and the harness avoids it.
- Sleep: the Mac that runs Docker slept for 450 s during the first full run (`2026-11-04T07-02-25.262Z-152ab6df-cb7d-459f-9334-71b5a515f840`) and for 86 s during a smoke run (`2026-11-04T07-51-48.111Z-e9c185b6-16b4-4b7e-9353-511aba20a25a`). Each time E5.a.closePages failed with gaps of 113 s to 479 s, and those runs were discarded; every other cell of both runs passed, E5.b.disconnectForget excepted. The recorded runs had no sleep (`pmset -g log`).

## What it means for the plan

- Decision 11 records the pairing as P3.2 fixed it.
- E5.b.disconnectForget: the Downloads builds need a pair-again control after Forget, and need to lose the File System Access banner and join button. This is a dashboard change for the Downloads transport; P4.4 owns the pause policy after Disconnect and Forget.
- Stamps: a reader could bound a signal's `sequence` and `expiresAt` by its own clock plus `CLOCK_SKEW_MS` (and `PAIRING_TTL_MS` for the expiry), as `catalog.sh` bounds presences, so a writer whose clock stepped back is not silenced for the rest of its boot. The extension's presence horizon could also allow `CLOCK_SKEW_MS`, as `registry.json`'s does, so that a peer slightly ahead is heard at once. Both are P3.3 or P9.1 decisions.
- The bootstrap boot adoption mid-pairing belongs to P4.2's launcher measurement.
